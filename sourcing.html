Why here, why now

Five reasons brands are moving production to Portugal.

01 — Craft

Quality that punches above its price

Portugal isn't the cheap option — it's the good one. This is Europe's #2 footwear producer, the shop floor behind Prada, Hermès and Louis Vuitton, the world's #1 cork supplier, and Marinha Grande has been cutting world-class injection moulds for decades. Translation: tight tolerances, proper finishing, and suppliers who take pride in the part.

02 — Future-proof

A supplier base that's levelling up

These factories aren't standing still. There's roughly €22bn of EU recovery money (the PRR) pushing automation and Industry 4.0 across the country. So you're not buying today's capacity — you're plugging into a base that's investing in kit and capability for the next decade.

03 — Resilience

De-risk your supply chain

One factory, one ocean away, is a single point of failure. Nearshoring jumped from 42% to 56% of companies in a year for exactly this reason. Run Portugal as your primary, or as a parallel line that keeps shipping when your main source hits a wall — tariffs, freight chaos, a shut border. Redundancy you can sleep on.

04 — Sustainability

Cleaner by default

Making local means less freight, full stop. And Portugal's grid ran ~75% renewable in 2025 — hydro, wind, and a fast-growing solar base. Lower carbon per unit, a legit "Made in EU" story, and numbers you can actually put in front of your customers.

05 — Proximity

Close enough to move fast

Same-ish time zone, short-haul flights, EU market with no internal duties. That means faster sampling loops, smaller MOQs, and factory visits that don't eat a week. When something's off, we're on the floor sorting it — not on a 3am call.

And someone who's done it before.

A decade taking hardware from prototype to production — tooling, DFM, CMF, supplier management, the lot. You get the reach of an agency with the attention of one person who's accountable end to end.
